About

I build physics-informed neural networks and neural operators that simulate patient-specific blood flow in seconds, so liver cancer therapy can be planned faster and more precisely.

I'm a Postdoctoral Scholar in Biomedical Engineering and Radiology at UC Davis. I study how blood moves through the hepatic arteries, because in Y-90 radioembolization, a targeted liver cancer therapy, where the flow goes is where the treatment goes.

Over seven years I've built multiphysics models in COMSOL and trained PINNs, DeepONets, and geometry-aware neural operators with PyTorch, DeepXDE, and NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo, validating them against finite-element simulation and laboratory experiments.

How I work

Principles behind the research

Method

Models that respect conservation laws need less data, generalize better to new anatomy, and fail in ways we can reason about. The physics lives in the loss, the architecture, or both.

Data

No two hepatic arterial trees are alike. Simulations should start from the patient's own imaging rather than an average anatomy, because the flow split decides where the treatment goes.

Speed

A treatment plan cannot wait hours for a CFD solve. Neural surrogates bring flow predictions down to seconds, so planning can become interactive.

Trust

Every surrogate is checked against finite-element simulation and, where possible, laboratory experiments, so speed never comes at the cost of trust.
