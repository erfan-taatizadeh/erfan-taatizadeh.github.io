Research

I develop physics-based and machine-learning models that make engineering and medical systems faster to simulate, easier to understand, and possible to optimize. The common thread is coupling multiphysics finite-element simulation with modern scientific machine learning, so expensive simulations become fast surrogates for design and clinical decisions.

Postdoctoral research · UC Davis

Physics-informed ML for liver cancer therapy

Yttrium-90 (Y-90) radioembolization treats liver tumors by delivering radioactive microspheres through the hepatic arteries. How well it works depends on where blood flow carries those microspheres, which is patient-specific and hard to predict.

I build physics-informed neural networks in NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo that solve the steady and pulsatile Navier–Stokes equations on a patient-specific hepatic arterial tree with 46 outlets, validated against COMSOL CFD across 21 benchmarked design variants. The predicted per-outlet flow split feeds a dosimetry pipeline that places microspheres and computes a 3D absorbed-dose map.

Postdoctoral research · UC Davis

Geometry-aware neural operators

To avoid a new CFD solve for every geometry, I train GINOT, a graph-informed neural operator that extends DeepONet with a MeshGraphNet branch and cross-attention. Built on NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo and trained on COMSOL CFD for 18 branching vessel geometries, it predicts the 3D velocity and pressure field from a vessel surface mesh in a single forward pass. Improving its accuracy on geometries it has never seen is the current focus.

The trained operator powers an interactive digital-twin app: pick or upload a vessel geometry, set the inlet velocity, and explore the predicted 3D flow and pressure.

PhD research · University of British Columbia · with Dr. John Madden

Conducting-polymer actuators for tactile feedback

Conducting-polymer actuators are soft artificial muscles that bend as ions move in and out of a polymer. I developed ultrathin, flexible tri-layer actuators with PEDOT:PSS electrodes on a PVDF membrane, designed to indent the fingertip at low voltage (under 2 V). I built hybrid analytical and COMSOL finite-element models to predict their response across designs and operating conditions, and characterized devices with electrochemical impedance spectroscopy, dynamic mechanical analysis, and tensile testing.

The goal was actuators fast and reliable enough for tactile feedback in healthcare and VR/AR. Alongside this, I designed and prototyped a low-cost silicone negative-pressure ventilator (NSERC Alliance funded) and co-prepared a CAD$24M NSERC Alliance grant proposal for spinal cord injury recovery research.

MASc research · University of British Columbia · with Dr. Mina Hoorfar

Acoustofluidics for exosome isolation

I developed lab-on-a-chip devices that use standing surface acoustic waves to separate micron- and nano-scale particles, aimed at isolating extracellular vesicles such as exosomes for biomedical research.

The work combined cleanroom microfabrication (interdigital transducers, photolithography, PVD) with COMSOL multiphysics modeling and experimental validation.

Postdoctoral research · UBC · Safe Water Optics

Optical modeling for UV-C water purification

I modeled UV-C disinfection systems by combining ray tracing in LightTools and Zemax OpticStudio with PINN-based optical modeling and laboratory validation, to optimize irradiance and system design.

Undergraduate research and collaborations

Tumor transport and imaging

As an undergraduate researcher, I wrote MATLAB image-processing code that detects blood vessels in images for COMSOL drug-delivery simulations in normal and cancerous tissue. I later co-authored image-based models of drug transport through heterogeneous tumor vasculature and of PET tracer distribution with the microvasculature included.
